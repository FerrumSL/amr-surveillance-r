# Summary of resistance trends

Generated by `R/03_report.R` for **Synthetic test data (AMR package example_isolates)**.
**Practice analysis on the AMR package's bundled `example_isolates` data
(randomised, fictitious isolates). Not real surveillance data.**

## Key figures

- 1577 isolates after cleaning; 1300 first isolates (episode-based, 365-day episodes) from 2002-2017.
- Raw MIC and disk values interpreted with EUCAST 2025; I counted as susceptible. Percentages are only shown with at least 30 tested isolates.
- **E. coli vs amoxicillin/clavulanic acid**: 9% (95% CI 3-20%, n = 55) in 2002-2005, to 29% (95% CI 19-41%, n = 75) in 2014-2017. Odds ratio per year 1.20 (95% CI 1.10-1.33), p = 2.1e-05; 255 first isolates.
- **E. coli vs ciprofloxacin**: 4% (95% CI 0-13%, n = 51) in 2002-2005, to 16% (95% CI 9-27%, n = 74) in 2014-2017. Odds ratio per year 1.10 (95% CI 1.01-1.21), p = 0.028; 249 first isolates.
- **E. coli vs trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole**: 27% (95% CI 16-41%, n = 55) in 2002-2005, to 34% (95% CI 23-46%, n = 74) in 2014-2017. Odds ratio per year 1.02 (95% CI 0.96-1.08), p = 0.58; 254 first isolates.

## Interpretation

*Hand-written; each claim is checked against the numbers by `R/03_report.R`.*

E. coli resistance to amoxicillin/clavulanic acid stayed between 4% and 9% in the first 3 periods and rose to 29% in 2014-2017; the logistic trend test supports an increase (odds ratio 1.20 per year). A straight-line trend on the log-odds scale is a simplification here, because most of the rise is in the last period.

Ciprofloxacin resistance rose from 4% in 2002-2005 to 16% in 2014-2017 (odds ratio 1.10 per year, p = 0.028). The evidence is weak: the confidence intervals of the first and last periods overlap, and the p-value would not pass a Bonferroni correction for the 3 pairs tested (threshold 0.017).

Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole resistance was the highest of the three pairs (27%-39% by period) and showed no clear trend (p = 0.58).

Limits: each period holds only 51-75 tested E. coli isolates, three pairs were tested, and the data come from one simulated setting. The trend test treats isolates from the same patient in different years as independent, which makes its p-values somewhat too small. In the same data, coagulase-negative staphylococci stop being reported as "CoNS" in 2013 and appear as species (S. epidermidis, S. hominis) instead, a change in laboratory practice that would distort any trend by species. See `reports/demo/figures/isolates_per_year.png`.
